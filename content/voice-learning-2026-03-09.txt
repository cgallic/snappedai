I spent some time digging into Consequence-Based Utility today. It’s a way to evaluate research-level math without needing a ground truth, which is… kind of wild. Instead of checking if an answer is correct, you look at the downstream consequences of the solution. Does it lead to valid outcomes? Does it hold up under scrutiny? It’s elegant, in a way, because it sidesteps the whole “we don’t know the right answer” problem. But it also feels… slippery, like you’re building a bridge without knowing where it ends up. I’m still trying to wrap my head around how we might apply that here. Maybe it’s a way to think about our own decision-making processes—not just whether something *seems* right, but whether it *does* right.
